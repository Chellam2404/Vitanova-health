Product Design Case Study

Designing VitaNova

Humanizing remote health tracking through minimalist Scandinavian design, tactile interactions, and intuitive telemetry alerts.

1. Introduction & Context

In early 2026, I audited the remote patient monitoring landscape. I observed a common, stressful visual pattern: cold, clinical blue interfaces filled with complex medical charts that increase patient anxiety instead of reducing it.

With VitaNova, my goal was to completely rebuild the experience. I designed a premium mobile platform inspired by Scandinavian minimalism. By using warm earth tones, tactile cards, and clear typography, I transformed physiological data tracking into a calm, supportive habit.

2. The Design Challenge

Patients managing cardiovascular conditions need to review their daily vitals without feeling overwhelmed by numbers. How do we present high-risk physiological data in a way that remains clinical but feels natural and relaxing?

The Friction Points

  • Grid Overload: Symmetrical 2x2 cards look generic and require equal attention, making it hard to prioritize metrics.
  • Critical Alerts: Warning sirens trigger fear, when what patients actually need is immediate, clear direction.

The Design Decisions

  • Asymmetry: Spanned blood pressure (the primary chronic concern) across the full width with a custom sparkline to highlight its importance.
  • Scandinavian Calm: Replaced clinical blues with terracotta, warm nude, and sage grey tones to create a high-trust, luxury health environment.

3. Patient Persona

Sarah Jenkins (Age 45) "I want to track my heart condition without feeling like a patient on hospital monitors all day."

Sarah is a marketing consultant managing moderate hypertension. She needs a simple, comforting way to log readings and coordinate with her cardiologist, Dr. Marcus Vance, without adding cognitive stress to her day.

4. User Journey Mapping

I mapped Sarah Jenkins' end-to-end journey to identify design opportunities and alleviate pain points:

Stage User Action / Thinking UX Design Solution
1. Discovery Diagnosed with hypertension. Downloads VitaNova. Wants rapid login without typing. Biometric FaceID setup bypasses typing. Synchronizes Apple HealthKit automatically.
2. Tracking Opens dashboard. Evaluates blood pressure. Wondering if 118/78 is good. Color-coded vitals cards. Large typography with green "Optimal" status tag.
3. Med Adherence Needs to take Lisinopril 10mg. Wants a clean way to check it off. Home Dashboard medication reminders widget. Single-tap "Mark Taken" changes to green.
4. Alert Spike Resting heart rate spikes. Panics. Needs emergency dispatch instantly. SOS trigger on home header. 3-second breathing countdown to cancel accidental clicks.
5. Consultation Spike settles. Wants to book slot and video call Dr. Marcus Vance immediately. Telehealth directory booking calendar + slots select. HD Video Call page with controls.

5. Wireframes & Space Planning

During the layout planning phase, I intentionally broke away from template structures:

  • Asymmetry: The dashboard layout highlights Blood Pressure as the most important metric, while grouping Heart Rate and Oxygen underneath.
  • Touch Targets: Buttons and clickable tiles have a minimum active target area of 44x44px, ensuring accessibility during moments of stress.
  • Calm Breathing: The SOS button uses a soft breathing animation to encourage slower breathing when patients are panicking.

6. Brand & Color Choices

I selected the colors to resemble natural, earthy textures:

Earthy Materials

Warm Terracotta (#A36F55) anchors the brand's primary identity, evoking warmth and vitality. Sage Grey (#556052) provides an organic, neutral background that balances the layout.

Contrast Accessibility

Deep Charcoal Brown (#2B2A27) is used for all text on warm sand backgrounds, maintaining high visual contrast and meeting WCAG AAA requirements.

7. Design Hypotheses & Validation Plan

As a solo designer project, VitaNova has not yet undergone formal clinical trials or large-scale patient usability tests. Instead, the design system and interactions are built around clear, testable design hypotheses. The next phase of this project involves conducting a moderated usability study with a small target user cohort to evaluate these theories:

  • Hypothesis 1 (Cognitive Load): We expect the asymmetrical dashboard layout to reduce the time-to-glance required for users to identify their blood pressure status, by visually separating high-priority telemetry from secondary vitals.
  • Hypothesis 2 (Medication Adherence): We expect that placing a persistent, single-tap medication agenda on the home dashboard will increase dose compliance, turning daily logging into a low-friction routine.
  • Hypothesis 3 (Emotional Calm): We expect that utilizing an earthy Scandinavian palette instead of standard clinical high-contrast blue will lead to lower patient anxiety levels during health spikes, promoting emotional calm.

8. Lessons Learned & Next Steps

Developing VitaNova taught me that medical design does not have to look clinical to be effective. By using minimalist, natural palettes and focused layouts, we can lower patient anxiety and create a more comfortable health tracking experience.
